Dolphin watching in Málaga on a private boat

We go out first thing, while the sea is calm, to look for dolphins a few miles offshore. One boat, your group, and the engine off when they show up.

How we look for dolphins

Early in the morning the sea is usually flatter and fins are easier to spot. We head a few miles offshore and stop to look around.

When they show up we slow down, cut the engine if they come close and let them decide. We never chase them and keep the distance set by Spain’s cetacean protection rules. With luck, they swim alongside the boat.

They are wild animals: a sighting cannot be guaranteed. On the way back, if you like, we stop in a cove for a swim.

How it usually goes

  1. Departure at 8:30 on a calm sea
  2. Cruise offshore looking for signs
  3. Watching with the engine idling or off
  4. A swim in a cove on the way back, if you like

Frequently asked questions

Are dolphins guaranteed?

No. They are wild animals and do not always appear. Not seeing them does not entitle you to a refund, because the trip goes ahead anyway.

Why so early?

First thing in the morning the sea is usually calmer and fins are easier to see.

Can we get close to them?

We approach slowly, never chase them and keep the legal distance. If they come to us, we stop the engine.

Can children come?

Yes, any age, wearing a life jacket when the skipper says so and always with an adult.

What if the weather is bad?

The skipper decides whether it is safe to go out, based on the forecast and the sea. If a trip is cancelled because of the weather, you choose between another date or a full refund of the deposit.

How do I pay?

When you book you pay a €20 per person deposit online, which comes off the total. The rest is paid in cash on the day, before boarding.
